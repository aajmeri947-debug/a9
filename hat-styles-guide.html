Hat Styles Explained

From the soft beret to the stiff straw boater, every classic hat has its own shape, story and best occasion. Here is how to tell them apart.

  • 5 chapters
  • 6 min read
  • Reviewed Nov 2026
No. 01
1

Fedora and trilby

Both have a pinched, indented crown and a band. The fedora has a wider brim, usually 6 cm or more, that can be snapped down at the front. The trilby has a shorter brim that angles down and a slightly lower crown, giving it a sharper, more casual look.

2

Straw summer hats

Straw hats keep you cool and shaded.

  • Panama: a fine, light woven straw, usually in a fedora shape.
  • Boater: a stiff straw with a flat crown and flat brim, often with a striped band.
  • Sun hat: a wide, soft brim for maximum shade.
3

Soft hats: beret and cloche

A beret is a soft, round, flat hat of felted wool, worn tilted to one side. The cloche, a bell-shaped hat from the 1920s, fits close to the head with a small downturned brim and suits short or tucked-up hair.

4

Structured classics

The bowler has a hard, rounded crown and a short, curled brim. The pillbox is a small, brimless hat with a flat top and straight sides, often worn at weddings and formal occasions, pinned in place rather than pulled down.

5

Choosing for the occasion

Felt hats suit autumn and winter; straw suits spring and summer. For formal events, choose structured shapes and neat bands. For everyday wear, softer shapes such as berets and wide-brim hats feel relaxed and easy.
